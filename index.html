How to build a set ▾
  1. Pick the slate. Classic is 8 spots under $50,000. Showdown is captain plus 5. The captain row is already priced at 1.5×, and the projection is scaled the same way.
  2. Start from a preset. GPP Leverage is the 20-max default. Milly is 150-max. Cash Floor is one lineup that spends the cap.
  3. Set exposure on the players you care about. Lock is every lineup. Core is 30–40%, High 20–30%, Mid 15–20%, Low 5–10%. Limit is for a play the data likes that you do not: still legal when a build needs the salary or the spot, but capped near 12% and scored behind comparable plays. Fade removes them. Min % and Max % fill in from that choice. The global max still caps everyone except locks.
  4. Use one point-guard stack, two or three players from that team. A five-man stack is not the edge. Bring-back adds a score bonus. It does not throw a lineup out.
  5. Run it, then read the review. Salary left over $800 on a classic slate, or over $1,800 on showdown, a chalky ownership lean, or a point guard with no teammate is a set to rebuild before you download. This is a pre-lock build. Late-swap if the starting lineup changes.

Optimizer settings ▾
Game stack and showdown shape
Min minutes, Min FP, and Min value leave anyone under that number out of the pool and the build. 0 means no floor. A lock still stays. Only at or above drops every player in a game under the O/U cutoff. Limit keeps those games, but a lineup can take at most two players from them when the rest of the slate can fill the roster. Prefer high totals only nudges. It does not ban the lower game.
Groups & stacks ▾
A stack is forced on the percent of lineups you set, and those players ignore the exposure cap. A group is conditional: use nobody from it, or at least the number you set. It is never “at most.”

What the tags mean

Extreme Core — 7x, 30 or more minutes, a high game total, and a good matchup. The first name you build around.

Core Play — 7x. The price is strong. It is not Extreme unless the minutes, total, and matchup are also there.

High Exposure — 5.2x or better with 28 or more minutes. A stud you can live with in a lot of lineups.

Mid Exposure — 4.6x to the stud range, with a real minute role. Normal price on this sport.

Low Exposure — 4x and playable. Fine as a one-off, not a priority.

Limit — the rate looks good, the role does not. Questionable, or a big per-minute number on short minutes. Use him when the salary needs it.

Fade — out, or the price does not pay. Also a listed projection that has no minutes average and no game-log points behind it.

Two tags on purpose. The first is the role. The second is how often to roster him. Hover either one for the reason on that player.
